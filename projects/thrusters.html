The Problem

In the first revision of the Arc Sport, there was a support bracket for the stern thruster motor. However, the support bracket took over 25 hours to assemble and install as it was made of sheet metal. My task was to design a test that quantatively illustrates how much load the thruster interface can withstand before yield and ultimate failure. During the project, additional scope was added to test the bow thruster motor too.

What I Did

Through this project, I was able to design an experiment from initial planning to data analysis.

  • Collected information regarding scope of test, specifically what the test should prove, which system is being validated, etc. Involved coordination with the Vehicle Engineering team
  • Designed stern thruster fixture to accurately simulate thruster motor; ensured load is applied at the CG of the motor perpendicular to the ground to simulate worst-case loading
  • Considered manufacturability and material selection of fixture, adding gussets for welds and calculating factor of safety
  • Built test instrumentation setup involving a load sensor, hydraulic jack, and turnbuckle; calculated instrumentation stack-up to ensure no stroke out.
Outcome
From my test, I was able to show that the stern thruster system begins to yield ~400 lbf, which is much lower than the maximum load it experiences on the boat. As a result, the support bracket was removed, thus saving 25 labour hours per boat. As I was able to finish the test 2 weeks earlier than expected, the scope expanded to design a similar test for the bow thruster. The bow thruster test was quite similar, however, required more knowledge of fiberglass layup and adhesion strategies.
